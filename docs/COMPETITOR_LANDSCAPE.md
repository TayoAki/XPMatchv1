# Competitor landscape: who they are, what they earn, who bought them, what users say

Compiled September 21, 2026 from five research files under `docs/research/competitors/` (A core AI
planners, B second-tier and long-tail AI planners, C organizers and group apps, D taste, creator,
done-for-you and advisor tools, E incumbents and the deal record). Those files hold the full record:
every price read from a live page on September 21, every quote with its URL, every estimate with its
method. This document is the synthesis. First of three working documents under `docs/BUSINESS_PLAN.md`;
the others are `docs/REVENUE_MODEL.md` and `docs/OPERATING_SYSTEM.md`.

**How to read the numbers.** Revenue is reported by almost nobody in this market. Every revenue figure
below is an estimate unless it says "reported", with a confidence label (High, Medium, Low) and the
method in the research file. Reddit blocks fetching, so Reddit quotes are verbatim search-index
snippets tied to the thread URL. Nothing here is invented; where nothing was found the cell says so.

## 3. Profiles that matter most to XPMatch

Each card: what it is, how it makes money, what users say, and the one thing to take. Quotes are
verbatim and sourced in the research files.

**Mindtrip.** Chat-first planner with maps, Start Anywhere import, hour-by-hour days, in-chat booking,
group chats, a creator program and a B2B line for hotels and destinations. Money: bookings and B2B;
no consumer subscription. Users like the polish ("UX/UI is absolutely brilliant, simple and sleek")
and real bookability ("these are actual booking links with live prices"). Users dislike slow or
failed generation, prices that are 20–30% off reality, and that it "can feel stronger at organizing
options than adjudicating hard trade-offs". Take: deciding, not collecting, is the open gap.

**Layla (Expedia).** Conversational agent with video-rich suggestions, day-by-day plans, bookings
through Booking.com, Skyscanner and GetYourGuide, and human experts in Premium. Money: Premium
$49.99/year plus commissions. Users like that it "actually did the booking part instead of dumping me
back to google" and that it is "way easier hands down" than ChatGPT. Users dislike errors on long
trips ("By far the most error prone AI on the market!"), the paywall "on the part you actually want",
and auto-renew. Take: 40% of its users start without a destination; the price anchor is $49.99.

**Wanderlog.** The itinerary-and-map organizer with real-time co-editing, expense split, reservation
import and a basic AI assistant. Money: Pro $39.99/year and affiliate deals. Users like that it kept
"12 different hotels, several activities, trains" in order and the desktop drag-and-drop. Users
dislike the upsell pop-ups, the forced trial, performance on large trips and the weak AI ("You'll be
doing most of the work manually"). Take: the board benchmark, and proof of $39.99/year at scale.

**Wanderboat.** Sequoia-backed feed and map of food, drinks, things to do and hotels with AI summaries
and tour-guide narratives. Money: none yet. Users like the interface and the guide content; users
dislike chain-heavy picks ("recommended two Starbucks when searching coffee") and thin data outside
big cities. Take: quality filtering is where a taste profile with dealbreakers wins.

**Stippl.** All-in-one organizer with AI itinerary, budget, packing, reels and eSIMs. Money: Pro
€24.99/year. Users like escaping "multiple spreadsheets, apps, and documents"; users dislike crashes
and freezes and generic AI picks. Take: instability and generic picks are the two things to never
ship.

**GuideGeek.** Matador Network's free chat assistant in WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, licensed as
branded bots to 30+ tourism boards. Money: licensing. Users like speed and no paywall; critics call
it a GPT wrapper with a margin. Take: the destination-licensing line is real and copyable.

**TripIt.** Forward or scan confirmations, get a master itinerary with alerts; owned by SAP Concur.
Money: Pro $49/year and Concur bundles. Users like "no effort required" import and alerts that beat
the airline; users dislike auto-renewal "without so much as a warning" and wrong gate alerts. Take: an
export target, not a rival; never surprise anyone with a renewal.

**Roadtrippers.** Map-first road-trip planner with RV navigation and three paid tiers. Money:
subscriptions. Users like stop management and quirky finds; users dislike the repricing that locked
"all my past data" behind a higher tier and navigation that damaged a trailer. Take: never gate a
person's own trips.

**Tripomatic.** Map-first sightseeing planner with a 50M-place database and offline maps, sold back to
its founders for €150K in 2023. Users like all-in-one planning and offline maps; users dislike
timezone bugs and rigid search. Take: a POI database alone does not monetize.

**Pilot.** Group workspace with chat, voting, AI itinerary and hotel booking with group rates. Money:
commissions and retreat contracts. Users like having everything in one place and the support; users
dislike a clunky, slow web app and booking follow-through. Take: the nearest overlap on group trips
plus booking, but filter-driven, not taste-driven.

**Beli.** Restaurant ranking by pairwise comparison, friends feed, maps and recommendations. Money:
paid creator guides and a Supper Club, "minimal revenue". Users like rating with friends and the map;
users dislike the mandatory pairwise ranking and contact-gated onboarding. Take: the reference for a
taste profile, and proof that taste data needs a booking or fee layer to pay.

**Thatch (Mindtrip).** Creator guide marketplace that became Mindtrip's creator content. Creators
liked interactive guides over PDFs; creators disliked the AI-first pivot ("don't want to share it
with AI!"). Take: creator trust in how content feeds AI is the sensitive point.

**Rexby.** Creator maps and guides with a per-guide AI assistant and a published split (80/20, or
50/20/30 when Rexby brings the buyer). Take: the going take rate for a creator layer is 20%.

**Elsewhere (Lonely Planet).** Bespoke trips by vetted local experts for a $100 crafting fee and a
deposit. Users like hands-off detail; users dislike price opacity ("about 40% LESS than the price
quoted"), no itemized receipts, ghosting and support hours. Take: the incumbent for a concierge tier,
beatable on transparency and speed.

**Journy (Hopper).** $25 or $50 per travel day for a human-built itinerary; seven-figure revenue at
exit, then shut. Take: consumers paid per day for planning; the labour model did not scale.

**Fora.** Host agency with 15,000+ mostly new advisors, $299/year dues, 70/30 splits and an in-house
AI assistant, Via. Advisors like the training and the tooling; advisors dislike the splits and gated
perks. Take: the channel for an advisor seat, and a competitor building the same AI layer.

**Travefy, Tern, TravelJoy, Safari Portal.** Seats at $19–59/month, or $199–399 for luxury. Advisors
like polished proposals, client apps and live flight data; advisors keep asking for running cost
totals and quoting, stronger CRM in itinerary-first tools, an agent mobile app and lower payment fees.
Take: the presentation bar for an advisor product, and cheap wins that nobody has shipped.

**Google AI Mode.** The exact matched stay, things to do and places to eat loop, travel-time-aware, on
a map, shareable, with agentic hotel booking, for free. Take: XPMatch cannot win on inventory or
distribution; it wins on taste-profile depth, group trips and one opinionated package.

## 5. What the audience likes and dislikes

Themes across all 40+ products, with who earns the praise or the complaint.

### Likes, in order of how often they appear

1. **Speed and less mental load.** "saved me from a lot of stress and work" (Layla); "Chatting with it
   is so much nicer than doing 30 Google queries" (Stardrift); "I made a plan in like 20 min."
   (Perplexity).
2. **Everything in one place with zero effort.** "Just scans my email account and creates trips. No
   effort required" (TripIt); "escaping the juggling act of multiple spreadsheets, apps, and documents"
   (Stippl); "Everything is in one place, everything is connected" (Tern).
3. **Map and itinerary in one view.** "you can just drag and drop, see the map and itinerary"
   (Wanderlog); "the itinerary came with a map that marked all of the attractions and restaurants"
   (Tripadvisor); "The map is the best part of the app" (Beli).
4. **Real bookability with live prices.** "actual booking links with live prices" (Mindtrip); "it
   actually did the booking part" (Layla); "Not just another trip planner. This one books it for me!"
   (Otto).
5. **Collaboration.** "very easy to share and brainstorm plans with my partner" (Wanderlog); "The
   ability to share the trip with a collaborator has been helpful" (Tripsy).
6. **No paywall, no sign-up.** "free, no paywall i hit" (Mindtrip); "Completely Free No Paywalls"
   (GuideGeek); "It's genuinely like Google Docs for travel planning" (Wanderlog free tier).
7. **Memory of preferences and calendar.** Stardrift and Otto ("rooms that avoid train noise").
8. **A human when it matters.** Layla's experts; Elsewhere's "partners in the field are so hands on";
   Pilot's "Great customer service!".
9. **Craft.** "The execution is great and it deserves 5 stars" (Tripsy); Mindtrip's UI; Safari
   Portal's "beautiful photography".
10. **Training and community** (Fora): "thoroughly impressed by the training they provide".

### Dislikes, in order of how often they appear

1. **Plans that ignore reality.** "the travel times were completely unrealistic" (Copilot2trip);
   "geographically inept, and suggest destinations that are closed far too often" (Tripadvisor);
   "recommended two Starbucks" (Wanderboat); a recommended trattoria with block-long lines (Vacay);
   "16 Free entry museums in Corfu town... all were 10 Euros" (Wanderlog).
2. **Billing behaviour.** Auto-renew "without so much as a warning" (TripIt); "It auto-renews."
   (Layla); "The app forces a free trial" (Wanderlog); repricing that "locks me out of all my past
   data" (Roadtrippers); "no way to... unsubscribe" (Copilot2trip); "Really difficult to cancel"
   (Roadtrippers). This is the single loudest theme among paid products.
3. **"How is this better than ChatGPT?"** Roam Around, Copilot2trip ("nearly identical replies"),
   GuideGeek ("a GPT wrapper"), Wonderplan ("a template-based itinerary generator").
4. **Bugs, crashes and slowness on long trips.** Layla, Mindtrip ("took forever to generate anything
   and then said it failed"), Wanderlog ("Performance is the #1 complaint"), Stippl, Wanderboat, Pilot's
   web app.
5. **Collecting instead of deciding, estimates instead of prices.** Mindtrip ("stronger at organizing
   options than adjudicating"), Stardrift ("displayed prices are indicative, not held"), Kayak ("not yet
   a strong travel advisor").
6. **Trust and safety.** Tripadvisor's Which? finding; ChatGPT "may hallucinate the restaurant names,
   travel times or suggest to you things that have closed"; "I don't trust it to book" (Perplexity);
   Airbnb's support bot that "just cancelled my reservation".
7. **Paywall placement and thin free tiers.** "The paywall has sat on the part you actually want"
   (Layla); "The free version is basically unusable" (Roadtrippers); Tripsy's "limited free version".
8. **Platform gaps.** Apple-only (Tripsy), iPhone-only (Stardrift), web-only (Let's Jetty, WePlanify,
   Vacay), US-only paid plans (Vacay).
9. **Products that vanish.** Troupe ("folded quietly"), Inspirock ("the website just died and i lost
   all of the plans i made"), Journy, Umapped, Roam Around. Users of this category have been burned
   and want their data to be exportable.
10. **Done-for-you opacity.** Elsewhere's unitemized quotes, ghosting and support hours.

**Backdrop.** Travel-tech funding hit a record low in Q1 2026 (about $1B across 44 rounds), and an
M&A adviser's view is that big OTAs "aren't buying small things". Marketplace buyers of small SaaS pay
a median 3.9× profit (Acquire.com, 2024 and 2025); a small consumer-subscription travel app is
estimated at 2–4× ARR or 3–4× profit because of churn and seasonality.

**Implication for the exit.** The credible strategic story is team plus taste-profile technology plus
an engaged base, priced as talent and data rather than ARR; the credible financial story is profit at
about four times. Realistic acquirers are a mid-size OTA, an experiences marketplace, a card issuer's
travel arm, an airline venture arm (United and Capital One already back Mindtrip), an advisor platform
or host agency, or a media group; not the largest OTAs.

## 7. What it means for XPMatch

1. **Position against "collecting".** Every core planner and Google assemble options. The one-card
   package with a visible match score and a reason per pick is the only "decides" product in the
   matrix. Keep it the first screen.
2. **Reality is the moat users name.** The top dislike is plans that ignore hours, distances,
   closures and quality. The catalog, Routes legs, quality floors and dealbreakers exist; make them
   visible ("open today", "12 minutes on foot", "no chains").
3. **Billing without surprises.** Annual-first with a reminder before renewal, pause instead of cancel,
   no paywall on a person's own trips, self-serve cancel, refunds within policy. This alone separates
   XPMatch from TripIt, Layla, Wanderlog and Roadtrippers in reviews.
4. **Export everything.** Calendar, PDF, Google Maps list, and a data export on the account page.
   Orphaned users of Troupe, Inspirock, Umapped and Journy are a real, if small, acquisition pool.
5. **Booking with live prices is the missing capability that reviewers reward.** Link-out with
   affiliate tracking first, rates on stay cards next (`docs/REVENUE_MODEL.md` section 7).
6. **Advisor seat at $49 with the three things advisors keep asking for:** running cost totals and
   quoting, a client-facing package that looks as good as Travefy's, and an export that fits Fora's
   workflow. Fora's Via and Tern's AI guides mean the window is now.
7. **Concierge beats Elsewhere on transparency:** itemized proposal, a delivery clock, one named
   human, and the board as the deliverable.
8. **Creators:** an 80/20 split is the market rate; be explicit about how creator content feeds the
   AI, because Thatch's creators left over exactly that.
9. **Do not chase offline, RV navigation, group voting or flights.** Each has an entrenched owner and
   none pays.
