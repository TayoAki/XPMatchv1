# Founder interview — question bank

Derived from the interview sections of 18 Quiet Light Brokerage listings (content sites, SaaS, e-commerce, marketplaces, services, education; 2023–2026), rewritten for a business that has not launched. "You" is the founder. Ask in batches of 4 to 8 per message, one section per message. Skip anything already answered.

Tag every numeric answer [V] Verified, [E] Estimated, or [A] Assumed. When the founder cannot answer, propose a default, tag it [A], and add it to the Validation Scorecard.

Sections marked **CORE** are always asked. Sections marked **MODULE** are asked only for the matching business type. Questions marked ★ are the ones buyers in the source listings pressed hardest on; never skip them.

---

## CORE — General Overview

1. ★ Describe what the business will do and who the target customer is, in plain words a stranger would understand.
2. What legal entity will own it, in which jurisdiction, and who are the owners with their percentages? (These are the people who would sign an LOI or purchase agreement later.)
3. Do you operate any other businesses, websites, or brands? Are any related to this one, and will any share staff, cash, or customers?
4. ★ Share your background and the story behind this idea. What did you see that others did not?
5. ★ Why start this now rather than a year ago or a year from now? What changed?
6. What assets will this business own on day one, and what has to be built or bought? (Domains, IP, content, code, inventory, contracts, lists, accounts.)
7. What strengths does a founder need to run this well? Which of those do you have, and which are you missing?
8. Who is the ideal buyer for this business in three to five years, and why would they pay for it?
9. If you had to name the three most pivotal decisions in the first year, what would they be?

## CORE — Business Model & Revenue Streams

10. ★ List every way the business will make money, and the expected share of revenue from each in Year 1 and Year 3.
11. What is the pricing, and how was it set? (Anchor to competitor prices found in research.)
12. Which revenue streams have the highest margin, and which the lowest?
13. Is there seasonality in this category? Best and worst months, and by how much?
14. Are there geographic limits on where you can sell or operate?
15. What is the expected customer concentration? Could one customer, one channel, or one platform ever exceed 20% of revenue?
16. How do the revenue streams change as the business matures? What gets added, what gets dropped?

## CORE — Customers & Demand

17. ★ Who is the typical customer? Age, role, income, location, what they are trying to accomplish when they find you.
18. ★ What evidence do you have that these customers exist and are already spending money on this problem?
19. How many customers does the Year-3 revenue number require, at what average revenue per customer?
20. What percentage of revenue do you expect from repeat customers or renewals in Year 2? Why that number?
21. Which customer segment has the highest expected lifetime value, and which the lowest?
22. What would make a customer leave? What is the expected churn or non-repeat rate, and where does that benchmark come from?

## CORE — Sales Process

23. ★ Walk through how a stranger becomes a paying customer, step by step, with the expected conversion rate at each step.
24. How long is the sales cycle from first contact to payment?
25. Who does the selling? Founder, hired reps, self-serve, marketplace? What does it cost per sale?
26. How will leads be tracked (CRM, spreadsheet, platform dashboard)?
27. What is the refund, return, or cancellation policy, and the expected rate?

## CORE — Advertising, Marketing & Social Media

28. ★ What are the primary sources of customers, and what share of revenue do you expect from each (organic search, paid, social, email, marketplace, referral, outbound, partnerships)?
29. What paid channels will you use, at what starting budget, and what cost per acquisition or return on ad spend makes the model work?
30. What is the organic plan: SEO, content, community, social? Who produces it and how often?
31. Will you build an email or SMS list? How will you gather contacts, and how often will you send?
32. Who makes the creative, and who approves it?
33. Which marketing channel is most likely to fail, and what is the fallback?
34. Are there influencer, affiliate, or partnership channels natural to this category? Name candidates.

## CORE — Operations, Workload & Staff

35. ★ How many hours per week will each founder spend in the first 90 days, at month 12, and at steady state? Break the time down by task.
36. ★ List every employee, contractor, or agency needed, with role, location, hours, and monthly cost. Mark which exist today.
37. Which tasks will be documented as SOPs, and who writes them?
38. What communication and project cadence will the team run on?
39. How long would it take a competent stranger to learn to run this business? What makes it hard?
40. Which decisions or relationships will depend on a founder personally, and what is the plan to make them transferable?

## CORE — Technology & Systems

41. ★ What is the platform or stack (site, app, store, CMS, hosting), and why that choice?
42. Who builds it, who maintains it, and what does that cost per month?
43. List every software subscription with monthly cost.
44. Where are the domain, hosting, and critical accounts held, and in whose name?
45. What technical skill does the founder need day to day? What can be outsourced?
46. What security, backup, and data-privacy obligations apply?

## CORE — Financial

47. ★ What is the total capital required to reach first revenue, and to reach cash breakeven? Itemize it.
48. What accounting software will be used, who does the books, and will books be separate from other businesses and personal accounts from day one?
49. Cash or accrual basis? For subscriptions or pre-sold campaigns, how is revenue recognized?
50. What payment processors, and what are their fees and payout delays?
51. What is the expected gross margin, and what is the biggest cost of goods line?
52. What owner compensation is assumed in the projection? (This is the largest SDE add-back.)
53. Which expenses in the model are one-time versus recurring?
54. What documents would prove the numbers to a lender in three years? (Bank statements, processor reports, tax returns, platform dashboards.) Set them up now.

## CORE — Competition

55. ★ Who are the top three to five competitors? For each: what they charge, who they serve, what they do better than you will.
56. ★ What advantages will you have over them, stated in a way that could be checked?
57. Is the barrier to entry high or low? What stops the next person from copying this in six months?
58. Have any competitors been acquired, shut down, or raised money recently? What does that say about the category?
59. What is the biggest competitive threat over three years, including AI, platform-native features, and larger players moving down-market?

## CORE — Legal, Regulatory & Platform Risk

60. Are there licenses, permits, certifications, or regulations that apply? Cost and lead time.
61. Which third-party platforms does the business depend on (Amazon, Shopify, App Store, Google, Meta, Stripe, marketplaces), and what in their terms of service could shut you down?
62. Trademarks, patents, copyrights: what will be filed, and is the name clear to use?
63. Tariffs, import rules, sales tax, data rules: which apply?
64. Any legal exposure from the founders' prior employment or non-competes?

## CORE — Launch Plan & Financing

65. ★ Where does the startup capital come from, and on what terms? (Founder cash, partner equity, revenue-first, loan.) If a loan, what equity injection is required?
66. How much working capital is needed after launch, and for how many months?
67. What is the minimum first offer and first channel? What does launch week look like?
68. What is the 90-day milestone that says "keep going", and the one that says "stop"?
69. If there are multiple founders, how are equity, roles, and decisions divided? What happens if one leaves?
70. Would you consider outside capital, partners, or a strategic co-founder? On what terms?

## CORE — The Clear Path to Growth

71. ★ Identify three to five projects that would increase revenue in years one to three. For each: what, why it works here, rough cost and time.
72. Which has the greatest potential, and why?
73. Which is the least expensive, and why?
74. Which is the most expensive, and why?
75. What would you do with an extra $50K, and with an extra 20 hours per week?

## CORE — SWOT Analysis

76. Strengths.
77. Weaknesses. (Push for at least three. Concentration, dependence, founder gaps, unproven demand.)
78. Opportunities.
79. Threats. (Push for platform, algorithm, AI, regulation, and macro items.)
80. What would keep you up at night about this business?
81. What is the one advantage that would be hardest for a competitor to replicate?

## MODULE — Content / Media site

Use when revenue comes from advertising, affiliates, sponsorships, or programmatic on owned traffic.

- ★ What will the content be, who writes it, and how often is it published? Cost per piece.
- What is the SEO thesis: which keyword clusters, what current top results look like, why you can outrank them? Cite volumes.
- How many pages at launch, at month 12?
- What share of traffic will be organic versus paid versus social versus email? Target monthly pageviews and users at month 12 and month 36.
- Which ad, affiliate, or sponsorship programs will monetize the traffic, at what expected RPM or commission?
- Which topics or page types are expected to drive most revenue?
- How will video and social be used, if at all?
- What analytics stack (GA4, Search Console, affiliate dashboards)?
- How exposed is the model to AI search summaries and algorithm updates? What is the diversification plan?
- Will you ever use a private blog network or bought links? (Answer should be no.)

## MODULE — SaaS / Software

Use when revenue is subscriptions, usage credits, or licenses.

- ★ Pricing plans, and the expected mix of customers on each. Monthly versus annual.
- ★ Target MRR at month 12 and month 36, with the customer count and average revenue per account behind it.
- Expected monthly churn, and where the benchmark comes from. Expected net revenue retention.
- Free trial or freemium? Expected trial-to-paid conversion, and whether a card is required.
- Customer acquisition cost by channel, and payback period.
- Expected lifetime value, and the formula used.
- Product: the three features that matter at launch, and what is deliberately not built.
- Integrations or marketplaces (app stores, helpdesk marketplaces, Shopify apps) that bring distribution. Approval lead times.
- Stack, hosting and model or API costs as a share of revenue, and how they scale.
- Security posture, backups, tenant isolation. Anything an enterprise buyer will ask for.
- Technical debt you are choosing to accept at launch.
- Support load per customer, and who handles it.

## MODULE — E-commerce / Physical product

Use when the business sells physical goods on its own store, Amazon, Etsy, wholesale, or retail.

- ★ Product lines at launch, count of SKUs, and expected revenue share by line.
- ★ Landed cost, retail price, and gross margin per hero SKU. Where the cost quote came from.
- Suppliers: how many, where located, terms, minimum order quantities, lead times, exclusivity. Backup supplier?
- Which products depend on China or other tariff-exposed origins, at what tariff rate, and how pricing absorbs it?
- Inventory: opening order size and cost, target weeks of cover, storage location, who fulfills (self, 3PL, FBA).
- Quality control and defect handling; expected return or exchange rate.
- Channel mix: own store, Amazon, Etsy, wholesale, retail. Expected share of each and the fee stack for each.
- Average order value, expected repeat purchase rate, and the levers for both (bundles, subscriptions, drops).
- Review strategy: how reviews are gathered, target count and rating at month 12.
- Amazon specifics if applicable: category, Brand Registry, PPC (manual or automatic), target TACOS, stockout plan, badge targets, top keywords.
- Paid media: starting budget by platform, target ROAS or contribution margin, who runs it, who makes creative.
- Website conversion rate target and the CRO plan.

## MODULE — Marketplace / Directory

Use when the business connects two sides (listings and buyers, advertisers and audience, providers and clients).

- ★ Which side comes first, and how do you seed it? How many listings or providers at launch?
- ★ Who pays: listings, buyers, advertisers, or transactions? Price points for each.
- How is supply added and kept current? Manual, submission forms, scraping, API partners? Hours per week.
- What makes the marketplace better than a search engine result or a spreadsheet?
- Expected repeat rate among paying advertisers or providers; concentration among the top five.
- Sales process for the paying side: inbound versus outbound, cycle length, commission structure.
- Inventory of what you sell (placements, featured slots, leads) and how it is priced and tracked.
- Geographic expansion plan and what a new region costs to open.
- Platform-dependence risk (search traffic, registration platforms, social).
- Can the system be ported to an adjacent vertical later?

## MODULE — Service / Agency / Productized service

Use when people deliver the product.

- ★ What is the service, what is the deliverable, and what is the price? Retainer, project, or per-unit.
- Who delivers it at launch (founders, contractors, employees), at what cost per delivered unit, and gross margin per engagement.
- Capacity: how many clients can the current team serve? What is the hiring trigger?
- Sales: where clients come from, close rate, sales cycle, who sells. Founder time in sales.
- Client concentration: expected share of revenue from the top three clients.
- Retention: expected engagement length and reasons clients leave.
- Onboarding and delivery SOPs; what is templated versus bespoke.
- Tools and software per client.
- Liability, contracts, insurance.
- Can the service become a product or a course? What would that take?

## MODULE — Education / Course / Community

Use when revenue is courses, memberships, cohorts, or paid communities.

- ★ Curriculum or offer at launch, format (self-paced, cohort, live), and price points.
- Who is the instructor or face of the brand, and how dependent is the business on that person?
- Expected enrollments or members at month 12 and 36, and the average revenue per member.
- Membership churn or course completion rate, and where the benchmark comes from.
- Content production: hours per lesson, refresh cadence, who produces.
- Platform (LMS, community tool, payments), and its fees.
- Acquisition: organic content, YouTube, podcasts, partnerships, paid. Expected cost per enrollment.
- Community moderation load and who does it.
- Accreditation, exam-board, or IP issues with the material.
- What stops a student from getting the same thing free from YouTube or an AI assistant?
