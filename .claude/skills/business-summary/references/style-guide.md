# Style guide — how the source listings read, and what to keep

Observed across 18 Quiet Light Brokerage summaries (2023–2026). Keep the discipline; drop the sales gloss.

## Voice

- **Executive Summary and Key Benefits** are written in third person about the business. Plain declarative sentences. Every paragraph carries at least one number.
- **The Founder Interview** is first person from the founder, answering a numbered second-person question. Keep the founder's phrasing where it is specific ("I draw it in MS Paint and send it to the manufacturer" is better than "we have a streamlined design process").
- Short sentences. The best answers in the source set are two to four sentences or a bullet list.

## Numbers

- Always give the unit and the currency: "US$4K per month", "15–20 hours per week", "0.6% monthly churn".
- Ranges are fine and often more honest than a point estimate. "10–15 qualified demos per month" beats "many".
- Give the period: "trailing 12 months ending April 2026", "2025 revenue", "at month 12".
- In this document every number in the interview and the quickview carries a tag: [V], [E], or [A]. Executive Summary prose can omit inline tags if the Quickview table below it carries them.
- Percentages of revenue for every stream and every channel. Buyers in the source listings asked for this in every business type.

## Formatting conventions to copy

- **Key Benefits** bullet shape: `**Bold lead phrase:** one sentence with a number.` Five to nine bullets. Examples from the source set:
  - **High-Retention Advertiser Base:** over 75% retention for advertisers spending over $1K per year and over 95% for those spending $10K or more.
  - **Low Churn:** the lender product is particularly sticky; overall 85% gross revenue retention.
  - **Hard-to-Rebuild Integration Layer:** working integrations across six major support platforms.
- **Key Risks** use the identical shape. This section is new; the source listings scatter risks into SWOT and "Note:" lines.
- **"Note:"** callout at the end of the Executive Summary for the single biggest caveat. Source examples: "This business is NOT SBA-financeable." "The business is headquartered in Canada."
- **Section headers in the interview** are ALL CAPS. Questions are numbered continuously across sections (the source documents run to 100+).
- **Competitor table** columns: Competitor · Primary focus · Pricing · What they do better · Our advantage · Source.
- **Team roster** answer shape: Role · Location · Rate · Monthly cost · Responsibilities. One block per person.
- **Time breakdown** answer shape: task · hours per week, one line each, with a total.
- **Growth section** shape: numbered projects with a bold title, then three follow-up answers (greatest potential, least expensive, most expensive).

## Honesty rules (stricter than the source)

- "Available on request" and "Unknown" are not acceptable in our own document. Write "Not yet determined. Test: [what], cost [$], by [date]." and add it to the Validation Scorecard.
- Weaknesses must have at least three items. The best source SWOTs name concentration, platform dependence, and founder gaps outright.
- Threats must consider: search or platform algorithm changes, AI substitutes and AI-generated search summaries, a platform building the feature natively, tariffs or regulation, macro spending shifts, and key-person risk.
- Do not write "no competition". Write who the customer uses today instead of you, even if that is a spreadsheet.
- Do not project revenue in month 1 without naming who pays.
- Banned words unless quoting a source: disruptive, revolutionary, huge, massive, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, game-changing, passionate, synergy, leverage (as a verb), unlock.

## Length

- Executive Summary: one page. Financial Quickview, Key Benefits, Key Risks fit on the next page.
- Founder Interview: 50 to 90 questions depending on modules. Answers as long as the facts require, no longer.
- Whole document: 15 to 35 pages equivalent. The source set ran 16 to 68.
